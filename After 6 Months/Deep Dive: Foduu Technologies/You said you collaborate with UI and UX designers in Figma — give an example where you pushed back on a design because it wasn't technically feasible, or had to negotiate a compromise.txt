Yeah, this happened during the checkout flow redesign, actually right around 
the same time as the address tracking work.

The Original Design:
The designer had mocked up a checkout screen where the live delivery tracking 
map was supposed to be embedded inline, 
scrolling along with the rest of the checkout page - so as you 
scrolled down through order summary, payment, etc.,the map itself would scroll 
up and down with it, partially visible, almost like an Instagram-feed-style card.

Why It Wasn't Feasible:
Technically, this created a real problem. GoogleMap widgets in Flutter are backed 
by native platform views( 'PlatformView' on both iOS and Android ), and 
embedding a native view inside.
a scrollable ListView or CustomScrollView causes real performance issues — janky 
scroll, behavior, gesture conflicts between the map's own pan/zoom gestures and the
page's scroll gesture, and on some Android devices, the map would flicker or not 
render correctly at all when it wasn't fully in frame.

I actually built a quick prototype to test it before pushing back — I didn't want 
to say "this won't work" based on theory alone. I showed the designer a screen 
recording of the janky scroll behavior and the gesture conflict where scrolling the 
page would sometimes accidentally pan the map instead.

The Negotiation:
Instead of just saying no, I proposed two alternatives and let the designer pick 
based on what mattered more for the product:
i. Option A: Keep the map in a fixed, non-scrolling container at the top of the 
screen (a set height, like 200-250dp), with the rest of the checkout content 
scrolling independently below it.
ii. Option B: Make the map a separate, dedicated "Track Order" screen accessible 
via a button/card on the checkout screen, rather than embedding it directly.

The Compromise:
We ended up going with Option A — a fixed-height map at the top, non-scrollable, 
with a small "expand" icon that let users open it full-screen if they wanted a 
bigger view. This kept the visual intent the designer wanted (live map visible 
right there on the checkout screen) while avoiding the platform view/scroll conflict
entirely, since the map's container itself never moved — only the content below it did.

I also explained the why clearly, not just the what — I made sure the designer 
understood it wasn't a "Flutter can't do this" limitation in general, it was 
specifically a PlatformView-inside-scrollable-widget constraint, so future designs 
could account for that pattern without needing to loop me in every time.
