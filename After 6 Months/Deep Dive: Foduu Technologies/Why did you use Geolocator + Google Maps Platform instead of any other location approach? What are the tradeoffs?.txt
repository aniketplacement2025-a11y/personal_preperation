We used Geolocator for getting the device's GPS coordinates, and Google Map Platform
(Maps SDK, Places, Geocoding) for everything visual and location search related.

The reason is these two solve different problems — 
Geolocator only gives you a lat/lng number, it has no idea what's on the map or 
what that address is called. For that, you need a maps/geocoding provider.

Now here's the part that actually matters — the alternatives and why we picked what
we picked:

        ALTERNATIVE 1: Apple Maps / native platform maps
Why Not: Apple Maps has no direct Android equivalent, so you'd need to build two completely 
separate implementations for iOS and Android. Since Flutter's whole value is cross-platform code, 
using Google Maps Platform (which works identically on both) keeps one codebase instead of two.

        ALTERNATIVE 2: OpenStreetMap (via flutter_map)
Why not (usually): OSM is free and open-source, no billing account needed 
— genuinely a strong option for cost-conscious apps. But:
i. Its Places/search-autocomplete data is far less complete than Google's, 
   especially in India for smaller localities, plot numbers, or newer buildings
ii. Its address-component structure for reverse geocoding is inconsistent 
    compared to Google's standardized address_components
iii. For a delivery app, address accuracy directly affects whether orders reach 
    the right doorstep — so weaker geocoding data is a real business risk.
When you WOULD pick it: If the client wanted to avoid Google's billing costs and mostly 
cared about basic mapping in well-mapped urban areas, OSM is a legitimate cost-saving trade-off. 
This is worth mentioning to show you know it's not "Google or nothing."

       ALTERNATIVE 3: Mapbox
Why not: Mapbox is actually a very solid competitor — better custom map styling, sometimes cheaper 
at scale. But Google's Places Autocomplete + Geocoding accuracy in India is generally considered 
stronger, and since the whole feature depended on address accuracy, not map aesthetics, 
Google made more sense functionally.

The real tradeoff to mention (this is the strong part of your answer)
"The main tradeoff with Google Maps Platform is cost — it's not free at scale, 
it requires a billing account linked even during development, and pricing is per-API-call 
for Places/Geocoding, which matters for a high-order-volume app like grocery delivery. 
The upside is significantly better address accuracy and autocomplete quality in Indian localities, 
which was the priority since wrong addresses directly cause failed deliveries. 
So it was a reliability-over-cost decision, not a default choice."
